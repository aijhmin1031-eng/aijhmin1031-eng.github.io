Among all side hustles, **selling digital files** is one of the few you can
start with essentially zero capital. No inventory, no shipping, and a file
you make once can sell forever. This is the map I put together while actually
setting up my own store — the detailed stories follow in separate posts.

![Watercolor illustration of a cozy work-from-home desk with a laptop and coffee]({{ "/assets/hero_start.jpg" | relative_url }})

## What exactly do you sell?

A "digital file" is anything a buyer downloads and uses:

- **Clipart PNGs** — watercolor illustrations, sticker art. Bought by
  crafters and makers.
- **Templates** — menus, price lists, invitations, planners. Bought by
  small business owners.
- **SVG cut files** — designs for cutting machines. Bought by t-shirt and
  mug makers.
- **Fonts and calligraphy** — hand-lettering assets.

The key property: the product **replicates infinitely**. Selling 100 copies
costs you nothing extra.

## Where do you sell? Three kinds of channels

Having set these up myself, sales channels fall into three categories.

**1. Marketplaces (Etsy, Creative Fabrica).** Buyers are already there and
find you through search. In exchange, fees are higher (Etsy: $0.20 listing
fee plus roughly 9.5% in transaction fees) and onboarding involves payout
verification. Long-term, most revenue comes from here.

**2. Storefront builders (Payhip, Gumroad).** Your own store page in
minutes, with lower fees (Payhip's free plan takes 5% per sale). The catch:
**you bring your own traffic.** These shine as the destination link for your
social media or blog.

**3. Payout infrastructure (PayPal, Payoneer).** Not stores — the pipes the
money flows through. Nearly mandatory for international platforms, and
**verification takes longer than you expect.** Mine has been in review for
over a week. Apply first, prepare products while you wait.

## The order I would follow if starting again

![Animated 4-step order: payout accounts first, finish a product line, open a storefront, enter the marketplace]({{ "/assets/steps_start_en.gif" | relative_url }})

1. **Apply for payout accounts first** — the review time is the bottleneck.
2. **Finish one product line** — not one or two items, but a coherent set of
   5–10 products sharing a concept. A store needs shelves that look stocked.
3. **Open a storefront builder first** — no review needed, so you can
   polish product pages, descriptions, and covers against real screens.
4. **Enter the marketplace** — once payout verification clears, register
   your polished listings.

## Three things to know before starting

- **Copyright.** Never use famous characters or brand names. It is the
  number-one reason accounts get suspended.
- **AI disclosure.** If you sell AI-generated imagery, disclose it where
  platform policy requires. Hiding it costs you the account later.
- **Consistency is the capital.** "Zero capital" means you pay with time
  instead of money. One listing changes nothing; a catalog does.
