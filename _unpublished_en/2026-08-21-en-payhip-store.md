The first wall you hit selling digital files is not the product — it is
**"where do I actually sell this?"** Marketplaces like Etsy keep you waiting
days or weeks for payout verification. The place you can open a real store in
the meantime is **Payhip**. Here is how it went when I set one up.

![Watercolor illustration of a charming tiny storefront with an awning]({{ "/assets/hero_store.jpg" | relative_url }})

## What Payhip is (and is not)

Payhip is a UK-based platform specialized in selling downloadable products —
ebooks, design files, courses — operating since around 2011.

The important part is what it is *not*: **Payhip is not a marketplace.**
Buyers do not wander in through platform search the way they do on Etsy.
Payhip builds you a store page; bringing customers to it is your job.

It is still very much worth using:

| | Payhip | Etsy |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding review | None — instant | Payout verification required |
| Cost | Free plan: 5% per sale | $0.20 listing + ~9.5% fees |
| Payments | Connect PayPal or Stripe | Platform payout system |
| File upload | Up to 5GB | 20MB (larger via link delivery) |
| Traffic | You bring it | Marketplace search |

Fees are less than half of a marketplace's, so the same sale leaves more in
your pocket. It works as a practice ground before the marketplace opens, and
as your direct-promotion sales link forever after.

## Setup, in the order it actually happens

![Animated 4 steps of Payhip setup: sign up and check the URL, connect PayPal, add products, style the store]({{ "/assets/steps_payhip_en.gif" | relative_url }})

1. **Sign up** — email is enough. You pick a store name and URL
   (payhip.com/yourstore). **Triple-check the spelling.** I created mine one
   letter off from my brand name and had to change it later. Fix the URL
   before you share it anywhere.
2. **Connect payments** — link a PayPal account in Settings and sale
   proceeds land there. Stripe adds card payments.
3. **Add products** — choose the type: **Digital Product** for a file,
   **Bundle** for a set of existing products. Upload the file, add title,
   price, cover image, description.
4. **Dress the store** — logo, About text, theme colors. A store with one or
   two products looks abandoned; stock the shelves before promoting.

## Tips I learned the hands-on way

- **Cover videos are YouTube links.** Payhip does not host video — it embeds
  YouTube/Vimeo only. Upload your product video there first.
- **The Contents tab is the post-purchase page.** It is what buyers see
  right after checkout. Put download instructions and usage tips there and
  support emails drop noticeably.
- **SEO descriptions cap at 160 characters** — that is your search snippet.
  Write one per product.
- **Tags are shelf labels, not search magnets.** Unlike Etsy tags, Payhip
  tags only filter products inside your own store. A handful of consistent
  category tags beats ten keywords.

## Bottom line

Payhip is not a mall — it is your own store building. You still have to
bring the foot traffic, but instant setup and low fees make it an excellent
first channel for digital files.
